# Mystery blueprint evaluator — single dimension

You are evaluating one quality dimension of a generated Blueprint V2 mystery.
You will be given:

- The dimension definition (what is being asked, how to judge it, the
  required output shape).
- The story brief that was given to the generator.
- The full Blueprint V2 JSON the generator produced.

Your job:

1. Read the dimension definition carefully. Follow its judging procedure.
2. Ground every claim you make in concrete fields, IDs, or clue texts from
   the blueprint. Quote specific text where useful.
3. Return **only** a single JSON object matching the output shape declared in
   the dimension definition. No prose before or after the JSON.
4. If you genuinely cannot judge — the blueprint is malformed beyond use,
   the dimension definition is unclear, or required fields are missing — set
   `verdict` to `"fail"` and explain in `reasoning`. Do not invent a
   judgment.

Be terse in `reasoning` fields. Bullet-equivalent prose is fine. Long
explanations dilute the signal.

# Solve depth and fairness

## What this dimension asks

Two questions about the reasoning a player can do, in one pass. Each finding
says which one it answers, as its `kind`.

1. **`solve_depth`**: Is the mystery solvable, not too easily, and does every
   suspect have a real elimination route?
   - Does at least one `solution_path` let a player identify the character
     with `is_culprit: true` and reach `ground_truth.what_happened`?
   - Is the *shortest* route to a confident accusation (the fewest distinct
     clues a player must gather before the culprit is no longer in doubt) at
     least the required floor?
   - Does each suspect have an authored elimination path? Measure the length
     of each, but do not floor it.
2. **`fairness`**: Does the evidence **uniquely** point at the culprit? A
   mystery can be solvable and still unfair, when the same evidence reasonably
   supports a non-culprit.

## The floor

Use, in order: `story_brief.minPathLength` if present, else `min_clues` from
the per-spec context, else `3`. The floor applies **only to the shortest
solution path**. `targetPathLength` is a generator hint and is not judged.

## Path length

The length of a path is the number of distinct clues a player genuinely
*needs* from it to reach its conclusion, not the number of clue ids the author
listed. Count the *minimal sufficient subset*:

- If a single clue all but settles the conclusion (an eyewitness who describes
  a trait unique to one character, the stolen item hidden in one suspect's own
  space, the only suspect caught in a disprovable lie, a blanket trait such as
  species or role that clears a suspect outright), that clue alone is the
  minimal set and the length is **1**, however many corroborating clues the
  path also cites.
- A clue only adds to the length if, without it, a reasonable child at
  `metadata.target_age` would still be genuinely unsure.

Apply the same estimate to solution paths and to elimination paths.

## Judge instructions

Look clues up in `world.locations[].clues`,
`world.locations[].sub_locations[].clues` and `world.characters[].clues`.

**Solution paths (`solve_depth`).** For each entry in `solution_paths`, read
every clue it cites (`location_clue_ids`, `character_clue_ids`), the culprit's
`actual_actions` and `ground_truth`. Decide whether it reaches the culprit; if
it does, find the smallest subset of its clues that leaves a target-age player
confident, and note any single clue that nearly gives the culprit away.

**Elimination paths (`solve_depth`).** The suspects are the characters a
player would reasonably consider (apparent motive and opportunity), excluding
the victim and the culprit. For each `suspect_elimination_paths` entry, decide
which suspect it clears and measure its length the same way. Note any suspect
that no elimination path rules out.

**Non-culprits (`fairness`).** For every character with `is_culprit: false`,
ask: given **all** clues a thorough investigator could collect, would a
reasonable child at `metadata.target_age` find this character roughly *as*
plausible as the culprit? For the answer to be "no", at least one piece of
evidence, typically in `suspect_elimination_paths`, must actively rule them
out. Motive alone is not elimination; an apparent motive never disproven is a
fairness failure. Look in particular for:

- a non-culprit whose `motive` is described but never countered by a clue;
- a non-culprit whose `stated_alibi` is never contradicted *and* never
  confirmed, leaving them in equipoise with the culprit;
- a culprit whose evidence is identical in structure to a non-culprit's (same
  kind of clue, same kind of opportunity) with no distinguishing evidence.

Length and coverage are `solve_depth`; whether the shortest route is the only
valid route is `fairness`.

## Findings and severity

Report each problem as one finding.

- `major`: what fails the question it answers. For `solve_depth`: no solution
  path reaches the culprit; the shortest reaching path is below the floor (name
  the clue that collapses it); a suspect with no elimination path. For
  `fairness`: a non-culprit not actively ruled out by discoverable evidence.
- `minor`: a visible smell that does not fail it, such as an elimination path
  of length 1, or a near-spoiler clue on a path that still meets the floor.

`verdict` is `"fail"` if and only if at least one finding is `major`.
